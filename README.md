# Block Party Pizza Competition

South Tower District, Fresno. A nonprofit community pizza event and judged
skill competition. One facet of a multidimensional skill platform: the
platform carries the value, not any single game. Pizza proves the same rails
as the game facets: verified skill, voluntary stake, real economic
constraint, and reputation that compounds with practice. Long-term goal: in
10 years the best pizza cooks in America come out of the Tower District,
because practice under constraint builds that skill.

## The one price

Eaters pay **$5, all you can eat**. That is the only price in the event.

## Base layer (nonprofit, balanced books)

- Cooking is free. Ovens are provided by Aaron. Ingredients are provided
  free by the nonprofit organization. A cook invests time only.
- Cooks are not paid labor. A cook's benefit is eating for free and
  cooking with ingredients provided free. Tips are voluntary gifts from
  anyone, independent of prizes and independent of how well a cook
  cooked; a tip is a gift, not a payment and not a prize.
- Eaters and ingredient-bringers contribute at least **$5 net**: cash,
  listed ingredients, or both.
- The app publishes the necessary ingredients list with a value per item.
  Ingredients brought are credited at listed value against the $5. Bring
  less than $5 worth, add cash for the difference. Bring $5 or more worth,
  you are covered.
- Before the event, cooks post their ingredient lists online. Anyone who
  signs up to bring a listed ingredient gets that value credited. What
  helpers bring goes to the organization for the cooks.
- Eater money covers ingredients first. Any money left after
  costs goes to the cooks as prizes. Nobody takes a profit.
- The $5 works because the average eater does not eat a whole pizza.

## Competition layer (optional, on top)

- Entering the judged competition is optional. The base event works
  without it, the same way free play works without prize play.
- Category ingredient budgets are **maximum ingredient costs per pizza,
  not prices**. A competition pizza must stay within its category budget.
  Exceeding the budget is not playing fair, and the pizza is not eligible.
- Cooks may make any size pizza within the budget. Target size is 14 to
  16 inches, undecided. Size versus ingredient quality is part of the
  competition: within the same budget, a bigger pizza or finer ingredients
  is a choice judges may reward either way.
- Judged competition uses the BetCube paradigm. Before you begin building
  a pizza, you declare in the app whether that pizza is a competition entry
  and how much you stake on it. Each pizza is an independent choice: you
  may enter none, one, or any number of the pizzas you make, at any amount
  per pizza. Once committed, the stake is binding and must be followed
  through.
- The stake sets the size of the prize you can win. Skill decides who
  wins. A bigger stake does not buy a better score. There is no cap on
  prizes.

## Categories

Pepperoni, Vegetarian, Meat Lovers, Supreme, Cheese. Cheese is broad:
cheese pizza, cheesy bread, cheese dips and dipping sauces all qualify.

## Dough

Dough may be made from scratch or bought. Piemonte's Italian Delicatessen
dough is listed in the app at its real price as the known neighborhood
baseline (Piemonte's Italian Delicatessen, 616 E Olive Ave, Fresno).
Scratch dough is budgeted at its flour, water, and yeast cost. A cook using
scratch dough may use the savings elsewhere within the category budget.
A cook can win on recipe alone, and scratch dough can beat store-bought
dough.

## Judging

- Judging happens in the app.
- Every pizza is cut into six slices, as near to equal as reasonably
  possible.
- Each slice is judged independently by the person eating it. One slice,
  one judgment, because slices can vary.
- Each slice that is eaten counts with equal weight toward the pizza's
  judgment. A slice that is not eaten does not receive a zero value; it
  simply reduces the numerator by one, so the pizza is judged only on the
  slices actually eaten.
- Each slice is worth about $1, give or take. Six slices is about $6 of
  value, so a $5 eater who eats a whole pizza comes out ahead. That is by
  design: everyone comes out ahead.
- Eating more than average is covered socially: extra cash into
  ingredients, a friend who eats less, or simply being OK with eating more
  than average. If everyone comes up short, scale everything up to keep
  everybody happy.

## App and ingredients

- Participants RSVP in the app. Cooks RSVP as Cooks; anyone bringing
  ingredients RSVPs against a cook's ingredient list.
- **Cooks eat for free.** A cook who brings ingredients is reimbursed for
  them at their listed value. Tips are voluntary gifts from anyone,
  independent of prizes and independent of how well a cook cooked.
- An eater may eat for free by bringing necessary ingredients from a
  cook's list, credited at listed value up to the $5.
- Each cook's list states, per item, the credited value (which may be per
  ounce or per unit), any minimum quality or brand requirement, and where
  the item must be purchased to gain credit. If a cook states no brand
  requirement, any brand qualifies. A picky cook may exclude a brand; a
  cook who is not picky may accept cupboard items and improvise with
  them, which is part of the skill.
- Ingredients are valued at the time they are brought to the table, by
  barcode scan in the app, which determines both the credited value and
  whether the item qualifies at all. Items must not be expired. Cupboard
  items qualify when they match a listed item. Nothing in the design
  encourages anyone to break the law: cupboard contributions are things
  the person already has and that are not expired, and benefit purchases
  are the person's own lawful purchases.
- To keep it simple, there is a single frame of reference for value:
  Walmart prices, entered into the app at the beginning from the Walmart
  app, manually or automated; the entry method does not matter. The
  Walmart price is the credited value for an item regardless of where it
  was purchased. If a person paid more than the Walmart price, they do
  not receive full credit; they receive the Walmart price. If a person
  found the item cheaper than Walmart, they may keep the margin;
  monetizing the margin is allowed. The exception is items that cannot
  be found at Walmart, such as the dough, which is listed from its
  neighborhood source.
