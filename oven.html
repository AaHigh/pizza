What it does

  • 950 degrees. The oven runs up to 950°F, and a pizza cooks in about 60 seconds at that heat.
  • 15 minute preheat. Fifteen minutes from cold to 950°F, then it holds that heat.
  • Room to spare. A 17-inch cooking area that fits family-sized 16-inch pizzas. Our 8-inch competition pizzas have room to spare.
  • Multi-fuel. Wood or charcoal straight out of the box, or gas with the Karu 2 Pro gas burner, fitted in under a minute.
  • Digital temperature, front and center. The Ooni Connect Digital Temperature Hub puts a digital readout on the front of the oven, shows the oven air temperature automatically, and sends readings to your phone over Bluetooth. It comes with a digital food probe for checking meats as they cook.
  • Watch the bake. The extra large glass door is heat-resistant borosilicate glass with ClearView technology, an airwash effect that keeps soot off the glass, so you can watch the cheese bubble and the crust rise without losing heat.
  • Built to hold heat. A powder-coated carbon steel shell with densely layered insulation locks the heat in, so the oven keeps cooking all night.
  • Cleans itself. At these temperatures the oven burns off food spills and leaves the stone clean. The fuel tray comes out after cooking for easy ash disposal.

Monday night: how the oven runs

Monday runs on propane, provided. To keep the first night simple, the oven runs on propane, and Aaron provides the propane. No wood, no charcoal, no fuss on night one.

Insist on wood? If you are a good enough cook to insist on wood-burning, bring your own wood. The oven does wood and charcoal beautifully, and a wood-fired bake is on the table for cooks who want it, but on the first night the wood is yours to bring.

First firing. The oven gets a full 30 minute heat soak before the first pizza goes in, so the stone is properly hot from the very first bake.

How a pizza cooks in it. Launch the pizza, rotate it every 20 to 30 seconds so it browns evenly, and pull it in about 60 seconds. Launch, turn, timing: that is where the skill shows, and that is what the judges taste.

Outdoor oven, and it stays put. This is an outdoor-only oven. Once it is hot, nobody moves it. Give it clearance and let it do its job.

Why it matters for the competition

A kitchen oven tops out around 500 to 550 degrees and takes most of ten minutes to bake a pizza. This oven runs nearly twice as hot and finishes in about a minute. That heat is what makes a real pizza crust: fast rise, leopard-spotted char, cheese bubbling while the middle stays tender. When every cook works out of the same 950 degree oven, the difference between pizzas is the cook, not the equipment. That is the whole point.
